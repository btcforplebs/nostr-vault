    Using the app

    Messages and notifications

    Private messages are end-to-end encrypted, and your notifications are made by your own vault on your phone. No outside push server sees them.

    Where your messages are

    • iPhone and Android: open the Vault tab and tap the envelope at the top right. A red dot means something is unread.
    • iPad: Messages in the sidebar, with an unread count.
    • Mac: Vault in the rail, then Messages in the bar at the top.
    • Web: Messages on the Vault tab, or in the sidebar on a computer (shortcut g m).

    Write to someone

    Start a message

    1. In Messages, tap the compose button (New Message).
    2. Type a name, or paste a key: an npub, an nprofile, a 64-character hex key or a nostr: link all work, even for someone whose profile hasn't loaded. It says so when nothing matches, the key is bad, or the key is your own.
    3. Pick the person. Their conversation opens, with its history, and you write there.

    People you've blocked never come up, and pasting a blocked person's key says so. A conversation with someone you blocked shows no messages, and its message box is off.

    You can also tap Message on someone's profile or on their card in the WoT tab, or Message seller on a Marketplace listing, which fills in the listing for you. On the web, type a name or paste an npub and tap Open.

    Opening a conversation marks it read. Mark all as read is at the top of the list. Read state stays on each device.

    Photos and videos

    Sending a photo or video

    In a conversation, tap the photo button (Attach photo or video; web: Photo), paste a photo with Paste Photo, or drag it in on a Mac or iPad. On iPhone, iPad, Mac and Android the same button sends videos too, and so does the web app. Photos other than GIFs are re-saved, which removes their location, and a GIF's hidden extras (comments and other metadata) are stripped.

    Since 3.2.3 a photo or video in a NIP-17 message is locked: the app encrypts it on your device before it uploads, so the media server only ever holds scrambled bytes, and the key travels inside the sealed message. It goes to your own media servers, or to nostr.download if they refuse it. Videos are capped at 100 MB (Android shrinks a big clip first). A received PDF or other file opens too: on iPhone, iPad and Mac it shows as a chip, such as PDF document with its size, and a tap opens it. The inbox and alerts say Photo or Video, never the link. NIP-04 messages have no locked files and still send a plain link.

    You can select and copy text in a message on iPhone and Android. Links in a message can be tapped and open in the app's own browser (more). There is no way to delete a message.

    Did it go?

    Sending, Not sent, Try again

    A message says Sending… until a relay takes it. If none does, it says Not sent with Try again (on iPhone, iPad and Mac also Remove). A message is never shown as sent when it wasn't, and never shows twice, even with a slow signer. (iPhone, iPad, Mac, Android and the web.)

    • While your relays haven't answered, the inbox says Loading messages… instead of looking empty, and after about 10 seconds Your message relays aren't answering yet.
    • Names load in the inbox and the conversation header, instead of a short key.
    • On iPhone, iPad and Mac, what you typed stays as the conversation's draft when you leave it. Drafts are kept in memory only, so they don't survive a restart. A send that fails after you left puts the words back as the draft.
    • Switching account closes the open conversation and drops its draft, so you never write as the wrong account. (iPhone, iPad, Mac and Android.)
    • Reading older messages, a new one no longer pulls you down to the bottom.

    Encryption

    NIP-17 and NIP-04

    Messages are sent with NIP-17: the message is sealed and wrapped so relays can't see who it's from, who it's for, or what it says. A copy goes to you too, so your other devices see it.

    Some older apps only speak the older NIP-04, which hides the text but not who's talking. Nostr Vault reads both. On iPhone and Android, the bar above the message box says NIP-17 (encrypted); tap Switch to send NIP-04 (legacy) in that conversation. It goes back to NIP-17 next time you open it. The web app sends NIP-17 only.

    If you sign with a signer app, some messages may wait for it: "N messages are waiting for your signer to open them". Open the signer, approve, and tap Retry.

    Where messages go

    DM relays and who can reach you

    Your DM inbox is a list of relays (the DMs column under Settings → Relays → Relays, then tap Save). Other apps send your messages there. Keep at least two: with No DM relay people can't message you, and with Only one DM relay nobody can if it goes down. Each desktop Vault whose DM inbox switch is on is listed first, in your Desktop Vaults order, before your own entries.

    Direct messages and their files may travel over the web (HTTPS and wss), like on any Nostr app: they are encrypted end to end before they leave your device, so a relay or media server only ever holds scrambled bytes. Your phone reads your desktop Vaults' inboxes over the web, and over the mesh first when a Vault has both. Drafts, private notes and chats never go over the web.

    When you send, Nostr Vault uses the other person's DM inbox, and falls back to their normal relays if they don't have one.

    Someone delivering straight to your vault's own inbox has to be in your web of trust. Follow Distance under Settings → Your Vault → Who Can Reach You sets how far out that goes (more).

    Messages from people outside your web of trust whom you've never written to wait under Requests, at the bottom of Messages. They don't notify you and don't count as unread. Reply to one to move it to your inbox. Messages from people you've blocked are hidden.

    What happened

    Your notifications

    On iPhone and Android, the Vault tab opens on your activity: replies, mentions, quotes, likes, reposts, zaps and new followers. Likes, zaps and reposts on the same post fold into one line ("Ann and 3 others"), and new followers fold per day. Tap Mentions to see only replies, mentions and quotes. The web app has the same list under Notifications.

    Likes and reposts from people outside your web of trust fold into one "from outside your web" line, so strangers don't crowd the list. Tap it to see them all. They don't count toward the unread badge. Zaps and new followers keep their own lines.

    With more than one account, chips above the list switch between All and each account, and each chip shows that account's new count. A line for another account says For name. In the account switcher, each other account shows how many new things it has.

    Settings

    Notification settings

    Open Settings → Notifications.

    • Notifications turns them all on or off.
    • New Notes in Your Feed tells you when new posts arrive.
    • Sound picks the alert sound.
    • Show message text puts a message's words in its alert. Off to start: the alert says only who it's from.
    • For each account: Mentions, Replies, Direct Messages, Zaps, Reactions (hidden in Zaps Only mode), Reposts and New Followers.

    Only people in your web of trust can set off an alert. Zaps and follows always come through. A message alerts only if it's from someone in your web of trust or someone you've written to. Other messages wait silently under Requests. A new follower outside your web is counted ("3 new followers") instead of named.

    A message alert says who it's from, not what it says. To see the text in the alert, turn on Show message text (off to start). The in-app pill shows the text either way. On a locked Android phone the alert hides the sender too. The web app's alerts never show the text.

    How they arrive

    No push server

    Your vault makes the notifications on your phone. Nothing goes through Apple's or Google's push servers.

    • iPhone: iOS wakes the app in the background now and then to check. Alerts can come a little late while the app is closed.
    • Android: the vault keeps running, shown by the "Your Vault" notification (with a Stop Vault button), so alerts come right away. After a restart or an update, message alerts start again without opening the app, unless you sign with Amber.
    • While the app is open, new things show as a pill at the top instead of a system alert. When you come back after a while, one pill sums it up, like "5 mentions · 2 zaps · 1 DM". Android files pills you didn't see under "Missed while open".
    • The web app shows browser notifications only while a tab is open. Turn them on with the Notifications switch under Settings → Notifications, or Turn on when the app asks after you sign in.

    What wakes the app, and what to allow, is different on each device: How notifications arrive.
