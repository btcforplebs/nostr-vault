The basics

What a media server is

Relays only carry text. A photo or video is too big for that, so it goes to a media server instead, and your note holds a link to it. Nostr media servers speak a shared language called Blossom, so any Nostr app can read from them.

A photo on a Blossom server is named by its fingerprint (a hash of the file). The same photo has the same name on every server, so it can be copied between servers and still be checked.

Your account publishes a list of your media servers. Other apps read that list to find your files.

When you post

Where a photo goes when you post

  1. The app first saves the photo on this device, in your vault.
  2. It then uploads it to all of your outside media servers at once.
  3. As soon as one of them has it, the note is sent. The other servers keep uploading in the background.

If no outside server takes the photo, the post is not lost. It waits under Settings → Media Servers in Waiting to send, and sends itself when a server answers. Tap Try sending now to retry straight away, or tap the bin next to a post to drop it.

Posting a photo still needs a public media server. A photo that is only on your phone can't be seen by anyone else, so the app won't post it without an outside server.

Your list

Add or remove a media server

Open Settings → Media Servers. It sits in the Relays group, under Relays.

  • Add: type the server's address (for example blossom.example.com) in the box and tap the add button next to it. If you leave out https://, the app adds it.
  • Remove: tap the red button next to a server (a minus sign on iPhone and Mac, a bin on Android).

Under Suggested, tap Add next to a well-known server to add it. Prices and limits are set by each server.

Each change is saved and your updated list is published to your relays.

A Mac you set up under Your Devices → Advanced → Desktop vault by web address is used as a media server too. On iPhone, iPad and Mac it shows at the top under Added for You, and you don't add it by hand.

Your vaults on the mesh are listed only if you choose to: under Your Vaults on the Mesh (on Android, YOUR VAULTS), tap Add to my media servers. Linking a device doesn't list it. Nothing is published until you tap Save; leaving with unsaved changes asks first.

In the web app, the same page is Settings → Media Servers. Use Add and Remove, or add one from Suggested. Each saved server is checked when the page opens, and Test checks it again. After a change, tap Save and publish, or Discard changes. The web app uploads to the server marked first first. If you have never published a list, it starts from two default servers. It also needs at least one server in the list.

There is no button to reorder servers. In the web app, a server you add goes to the end of the list.

Copies you own

Mirroring

Mirroring brings your own photos back into your vault on this device, so they work offline and you keep a copy if a server goes away. It looks in two places: the file lists of your media servers, and the photo and video links in your own notes, wherever they are hosted.

  • Auto-Mirror Media (on the Media Servers page; under Offline Copies on iPhone, iPad and Mac) does it for you. It is off until you turn it on. It runs when you open the app.
  • To run it by hand, use Import media on the Vault dashboard (on Android also Mirror Now on the Media Servers page).

A run keeps to a few rules, so it never floods your phone, your servers or your signer:

  • Nothing twice. A photo already in your vault is never downloaded again. The app knows a file's fingerprint before it downloads, from the link, from the note, or from an earlier run. A photo you deleted from your vault isn't brought back.
  • Few signatures. Your own account saves into its own vault without asking your signer for each file: your signer is asked once per run. On iPhone, iPad and Mac, another account you are signed in to signs once per 50 files.
  • Once every 3 hours at most, counted from when a run starts, so reopening the app halfway through doesn't start another one. Import media always runs.
  • 200 files per run. The rest come next run, and the result says how many are left. A big backlog comes in at up to 1,600 files a day by itself, or 200 more each time you tap Import media.

Auto-Mirror only brings files to this device. Sending them on to your other devices is what full copy does.

Taking a photo down

Deleting a photo

In Media, hold a photo:

  • Delete from media servers removes it from your outside media servers, any server in your published list and the server in its link. It keeps the copy on this device and on your mobile or desktop vault.
  • Delete everywhere removes those copies too, and offers to delete the posts that use it.

The web app has only Delete from media servers.

Other people's photos

Media & Cache

When you look at photos and videos in the feed, the app keeps temporary copies so they load faster next time.

iPhone, iPad and Mac keep it simple: copies are kept for 7 days, and profile pictures are fetched ahead once a day on Wi-Fi. Autoplay Videos is under Settings → Feed, and Clear cache is on the Vault dashboard. There is no Media & Cache page.

Android has the page: Settings → Media & Cache, in the Feed & Display group. The web app has neither.

  • Autoplay Videos plays videos as you scroll.
  • Prefetch Profile Pictures loads profile pictures ahead of time for smoother scrolling.
  • Disable Media Cache stops saving copies, so media downloads again each time.
  • Keep Media For sets how long copies are kept: 1, 3, 7, 14 or 30 days, or Never (they don't expire). It starts at 3 days.
  • Clear Media Cache deletes the temporary copies to free space. Your vault and your media servers are not touched.

On Android there is one more switch, Use Local Blossom Cache. It loads media through a Blossom cache app on the same phone, when one is running. Uploads never go through it.

Across your devices

The Media tab

The Media tab shows the photos and videos in your vault. It is live: each file appears the moment it lands, from Auto-Mirror, a feed Mirror, Import media or another of your devices, with no pull to refresh.

  • Files you add on the Media tab (Photos, Files, or Magic Paste of a picture or link) reach your mobile or desktop vault and the Media tabs of your other devices, the same as photos you post.
  • With Keep a full copy on this phone on (the default), every file from your other devices shows here. Off, a file loads when you open it.
  • Delete everywhere removes it from your other devices' Media tabs too.
  • When you add media to a post, Pick from your media opens full height on Android.

From other apps

Uploading from the share sheet

You can send photos, videos, links and text to Nostr Vault from your gallery or any other app, using the system share button. Pick Nostr Vault and it offers what fits what you shared:

  • Picture or video post: a new post with the photo or video.
  • Keep a private copy: an encrypted copy only you can open, listed under Private copies.
  • Save for later: a bookmark, in Vault → Bookmarks.
  • A shared profile: Follow, mute or add to a list (Android: Open profile).
  • A Lightning invoice or address: Pay or zap (Android: Pay).
  • Send privately: a direct message to someone you talk to.

Plain uploads still work. The two phones handle them differently:

PhoneWhat happens
AndroidPick Nostr Vault in the share sheet. The upload to Blossom starts straight away, one file at a time, with a notification while it runs.
iPhonePick Nostr Vault, then tap Add to the upload queue. The files are added to a queue. Open Nostr Vault or tap its notification to finish the upload.

On iPhone the upload happens in the app, not in the share sheet. That keeps your key out of the share sheet, and a big video doesn't depend on the sheet staying open.

Bring your media home

Import media

On iPhone, iPad, Mac and Android, Vault Dashboard → Keep it full → Import media runs the same import as Auto-Mirror, by hand: it copies the photos and videos on your media servers and in your notes into your vault. Tap Import. It runs once by itself after your notes first come in, and takes up to 200 files each time.

  • Each file is written straight to disk as it downloads, so big videos come in without running the app out of memory. Big files go one at a time.
  • It checks free space first, and skips a file there's no room for.
  • It works for read-only accounts too.
  • The web app can't take files from your media servers; Back up media saves them to a file instead.

Keep watching

Picture in picture

Keep a video playing in a small window while you scroll or use another app.

  • iPhone and iPad: the Picture in Picture button next to mute on a feed video, or the PiP button in the full-screen and live player. A live stream reopened from the mini player can go to PiP too, and closing PiP leaves it playing in the mini player. The PiP window keeps clear of the dock.
  • Mac: the PiP button in the full-screen player.
  • Android: Picture in picture next to Full screen on a live stream, or the PiP chip on a feed video. Going to the home screen while a stream plays also opens it in PiP.
  • Web: the PiP button on the live stream player.

Your own device

Your device as a photo server

Your phone or Mac can also serve your photos to others over the mesh. That is covered on The mesh. It adds to your media servers and doesn't replace them, so you still need a public one to post.

The web app can already load photos over the mesh. The Load photos over the mesh switch on its Media Servers page turns that off. It is on by default.
